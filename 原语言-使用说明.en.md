# Arche · Guide

Inside the idea you bring, there is a small thing that doesn't change — take away every *how*, and you still want it.

This guide shows you how to find it, and how to come back once it has grown.

## The loop

```
①  Idea             Still vague, but there is a real want underneath
      │   say 「开始」 ("start")
②  Sketch           Written in your own words and put in front of you
      │
      │   say 「归原」 ("return to origin")   ←── if you already have something, enter here; skip ① and ②
      │
③  y                The want that is left when every how is taken away
      │   seal
④  Seed             One file, three things pinned down; cut whatever feels wrong
      │   grow
⑤  The real thing   Hand the seed to an AI and let it grow
      │   use it, hit a snag
⑥  Coming back
      │
      ├─ The seed was written wrong  →  rewrite that one sentence, back to ④
      ├─ The seed was right, the thing drifted  →  leave the seed, fix the thing, back to ⑤
      └─ The want itself changed  →  say 「归原」, back to ③, seal a new seed
```

The loop can run many times. Each time round, the seed gets sturdier, and you get better at judging.

## ① → ② Start

Give `原语言.md`, whole, to any AI that can read text, ask it to reply in English, and say 「开始」 ("start").

It talks with you, then writes your idea down in your own words.

If the idea is already clear, skip this and have the AI do it directly.

**What you do**: speak plainly; don't polish your words for it. When it has written the sketch, you judge in one line — yes, that's it; or no, it's this.

## ② → ③ Return to origin

The question is always the same:

> Take away this *how* — what is left that you still want?

What you can return to origin is not only an idea:

- the sketch you just made
- an old seed, or a seed that grew crooked
- a piece of code
- an essay or a document
- a half-built project that keeps getting less like what you meant

Whichever you have, send it over whole and say 「归原」 ("return to origin"); ① and ② are skipped.

How it asks, which layer it peels first, where it stops — it walks you through on the spot; you don't need to learn it beforehand.

**What you do**: answer honestly, and don't be afraid to overturn yourself.

Only you can say "that's it."

## ③ → ④ Seal

One file, three things pinned down: the question that doesn't change, how you'll know it's right, and which direction to try. It lays the whole thing out at once, and you cut.

**What you do**: read it through and judge by feel. Any line that reads wrong, cut it; go back and forth until it feels right. It will also ask you one thing on its own: could this ever be finished? Answer truthfully.

How you'll know it's right is written as "what has been reached." What you've decided must never happen is written in a form that can be reached too — "the original data is still there, row by row," not "don't lose data"; and never a vague "don't get it wrong" — write it as a ban and the AI will perform for you.

## ④ → ⑤ Grow

When you say it's final, this session is done. You now hold one file; whoever reads it next doesn't need this chat.

Whether it holds up can't be measured in this session — only after something has grown from it and you have really used it.

Then open a new chat and hand it to an AI to grow the real thing. That part is outside this language — the shape, the structure, the steps are up to the AI that grows it.

## ⑤ → ⑥ Coming back

There will be snags. They are not in the seed; they are in real use.

Before you touch the seed, if you'd rather not judge yourself: send the seed and what grew back together and say 「归原」 — it first asks whether what you want has changed, then reads the two side by side.

You can judge it yourself too. Reread the seed: the thing that went wrong — was it already written there?

**Already written** — then it isn't the seed's fault; the thing that grew drifted. Leave the seed untouched and go fix the thing. Write the thing's drift into the seed as a scar and the seed doubles in a day.

If it really wasn't written, ask one more thing:

> What changed this time — did it touch "the question that doesn't change"?

**Didn't touch** — that sentence was written wrong. Go back and rewrite it, and move to the next version. Rewrite only; never add a sentence beside it to explain: a seed that only grows will, sooner or later, become a spec.

**Touched** — then it isn't a rewrite, it's a new seed. Send the seed and what grew back together, say 「归原」, and run the loop again. This time you are returning not your daydream but something you have actually run once — more accurate than the first time.

## You only do three things

**Speak plainly, say when it's right, judge the feel.**

The rest — how to ask, how to guide, how to write — is its job. Sometimes it will push back and say why. That is discipline, not law; you can overrule it.

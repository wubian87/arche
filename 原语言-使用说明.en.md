# Arche · Guide

Prompts getting longer, while the project drifts further from what you first wanted?

Inside the idea you bring, there is a small thing that doesn't change — take away every *how*, and you still want it. Arche helps you find it and pin it down as a seed you can carry with you.

This guide only covers what you do. How the AI walks you through it is written in [原语言.md](原语言.md) — that file is for the AI, you don't need to read it. (It is in Chinese.)

## Start

Give `原语言.md`, whole, to any AI that can read text. Then look at what you have in hand:

**Nothing yet, just a vague idea** → say `开始：your idea` ("start").

**You already have something** → send it along and say `归原` ("return to origin"). What you can return to origin is not only an idea: an old seed, a piece of code, an essay, a half-built project that keeps getting less like what you meant — any of these.

Copy those two words exactly; they are Chinese because the file is. Everything else you can write in English. If the AI answers in Chinese anyway, just ask it to reply in English.

Not sure how to begin? Adapt this:

> Read the attached 原语言 and reply in English. 归原: this is something I've already made and now feel has drifted. Below: why I wanted it at first, what it has become, and what moment made it feel wrong:

Share only the part you are happy for it to read. If something is unclear to you, say it is unclear.

## What you do

**Say it plainly, seal it, judge the feel.**

- **Say it plainly.** Don't polish your words for it. If you aren't sure, say you aren't sure.
- **Seal it (封口).** "That's it" is yours alone to say. It will lay out candidates and tell you which way it leans, but it won't nod on your behalf.
- **Judge the feel.** The seed is laid out whole and you cut. Any line that reads wrong, cut it; go back and forth until it feels right. It is final only when you say 定稿 ("final").

The rest — how to ask, how to guide, how to write — is its job. Sometimes it will push back and say why. That is discipline, not law; you can overrule it.

## What you get

A seed, in one file: the want that doesn't change, how you will know it is right, which direction to try, a short note of background and warning signs, and a last cell telling the AI that receives it what to try first. Next to it sit two ledgers (version, retired); they are read only when the seed is being edited, not when something is being grown from it.

Want to see a seed before making your own? [examples/Candlebreath.seed.md](examples/Candlebreath.seed.md) — a candle that breathes with you, a few dozen lines. You can hand it straight to an AI and grow a candle; you don't need the Chinese file for that. (Its Chinese original is [烛息](examples/烛息.seed.md).)

Then hand the seed, alone, to an AI that hasn't seen this conversation and let it grow. What it grows, and how, is up to it — that part is outside Arche.

## After it grows

If what grew is wrong, don't touch the seed yet. Send the seed and the thing it grew back together, and say `归原`. It will first judge: did the thing drift, or was the seed not written right? If what you want has itself changed, that is a different seed.

## Good to know

- It doesn't promise to fix your project on the spot. It helps you pin down "what I want, and how I'll know it's right", so you can take that with you.
- Saying "feels right" doesn't mean the seed holds. You only find out after something has been grown from it and you have really used it.
- If the idea isn't clear yet, it is fine to stop at the question. You don't have to pretend it's clear just to hand in a seed.
- If you already know what you want and how you'll know it's right, and only need an implementation or a bug fix — skip this and have the AI do it directly.

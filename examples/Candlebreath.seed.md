# Seed · Candlebreath

This is a "seed" — not a spec, not a requirements document.
It settles three things — y (the want that doesn't change), acceptance (how you know it's right), and bets (which direction to try). "Air" is not one of them: it is background and drift warnings, not extra acceptance. The last small cell, "Grow": the AI that receives this seed grows it into something usable.
To the AI that receives this seed: your job is to grow something real and usable from y and acceptance.
Do what the seed states; structure, form and steps are yours to decide — don't copy the seed as if it were a spec.

## y
Keep a person company through a few minutes of breathing: one candle flame, rising and falling with their in-breath, hold, and out-breath.
How you know whether it has drifted: the body breathing along with it. Slowing down and wanting to go on — right. Feeling rushed and wanting to close it early — drifted.

## Acceptance (by priority)
1. Breathe along with it for three minutes and be held: the breathing really slows, the practice ends on its own, and you never wanted to close it early.
2. Open and begin: one file; opening it in a browser is the whole thing; phone and computer both; no instructions needed.
3. After closing it, the person is a little stiller than when they opened it.

## Bets
1. Bet on "flame and breath in step": the image is one candle flame; rhythm, visuals, language, whether there is any text at all — all left open.
   What would retire it: stance
2. Bet on "slow and empty": if it can be left out, leave it out.
   What would retire it: the slow, empty version makes people want to close it early more than the version with things added does.

## Air
The judge is the body, not the eye: a gorgeous flame loses to one breath that is truly held.
Drift signal: adding check-ins, stats, achievements, streaks — turning one breath into something that rushes people.
Drift signal: piling on particles, sound, effects — they are for the eyes, not for the body.

## Grow
Start by building a page with only the flame, letting it rise and fall with one breath — in, hold, out; then breathe along with it yourself for three minutes and watch whether your breathing slows and whether you want to close it early. It slows and you don't: a hit.
